The Challenge

What problem is
HungryHub facing?

HungryHub is a restaurant booking platform operating across Southeast Asia. With thousands of restaurant partners on their platform, their marketing team faces a critical allocation problem — limited resources, unlimited restaurants, and no systematic way to tell which ones are genuinely growing versus temporarily spiking.

🎯
No systematic prioritisation

Marketing spend was allocated without a data-driven framework to identify which restaurants have the highest growth potential and would benefit most from campaigns.

📊
Current metrics miss momentum

Looking at booking volume alone misses restaurants with exceptional growth rates but still-low absolute volume — the most promising emerging opportunities are invisible.

🔁
No way to learn from peers

There was no mechanism to identify what marketing channels and campaigns worked for comparable restaurants, making each campaign decision effectively a fresh guess.

📉
Poor ROI visibility

Without campaign uplift measurement across CRM, KOL, and Facebook channels, it was impossible to justify or optimise where to direct limited marketing resources.

Our Approach

How we solve it

We built a five-stage pipeline that transforms raw booking transactions, marketing campaign data, and Google Analytics signals into a prioritised restaurant list with peer-validated strategy recommendations.

My Contribution

What I built

I owned two end-to-end components: the NLP clustering pipeline that groups restaurants by thematic similarity, and the peer recommender system that surfaces evidence-backed marketing strategies from comparable high-performing peers.

Restaurants are grouped into 5 thematic clusters using a multi-source text pipeline. Rather than clustering on metadata alone, the model reads what customers actually say — then anchors those signals with restaurant name keywords and Google Places type tags at higher weights to ensure structurally obvious categories (buffets, bars, cafes) are placed correctly.

NLP Restaurant Clustering

01

Extract TF-IDF vectors from restaurant review text (1,000 features, 1–2 gram, weight ×1)

02

Extract keyword signals from restaurant names — "bar", "buffet", "cafe", "omakase" (weight ×10)

03

Encode Google Places API type tags — "buffet_restaurant", "cafe", "bar" (weight ×4)

04

Aggregate to restaurant level via mean vector, L2-normalise, run K-Means (k=5)

05

UMAP reduces to 2D for visualisation; (x, y) coordinates become the similarity metric for the peer recommender

06

Manual name-match override logic corrects structurally obvious misassignments

Peer-Based Strategy Recommender

The peer recommender finds the K most similar restaurants using the UMAP coordinates from clustering, then aggregates the historical campaign outcomes of those peers to surface evidence-backed strategies ranked by peer-validated uplift.

01

Filter peer pool to Rising Stars and Established Players with recorded campaign data

02

Compute Euclidean distance on standardised (x, y, gmv_per_view) features; apply same-cluster bonus (−0.5) and momentum bonus (−0.5)

03

Select top-K nearest peers; fall back to other segments if not enough high-momentum peers exist

04

Assign each peer a similarity weight = 1 / (distance + ε); closer peers contribute more to strategy scores

05

Aggregate peer campaigns by (strategy_name, channel): median uplift, median ROI, peer count, total similarity weight

06

Score = uplift×100 + ROI×50 + log(sim_weight)×20 + reliability_bonus×30; diversify across funnel stages
